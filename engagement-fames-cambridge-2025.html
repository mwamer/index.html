Dr. Wesam Amer Presents New Research on Hamas’s Digital Communication Strategies During the Gaza War

19 May 2025 · FAMES, University of Cambridge, United Kingdom · Invited Speaker

Dr. Wesam Amer presented new research at FAMES, University of Cambridge, examining Hamas’s use of Telegram during the Gaza War. Drawing on critical discourse analysis, social media affordance theory, and online ethnography, the studies explored how digital platforms are used to construct narratives, reinforce identities, and sustain communication during conflict.

Dr. Wesam Amer recently delivered a research presentation at FAMES, University of Cambridge, sharing findings from two ongoing studies examining Hamas’s digital communication strategies during the Gaza War (2023–2025). The presentation focused on how Telegram has emerged as a central communication platform within contemporary conflict environments and how digital media is increasingly intertwined with political communication, narrative construction, and information dissemination.

Drawing on critical discourse analysis, social media affordance frameworks, and online ethnography, Dr. Amer analyzed how Hamas constructs, circulates, and amplifies its military media narratives through Telegram. His research examined the platform not simply as a communication channel but as a strategic environment that shapes interactions between organizations, supporters, audiences, and broader information ecosystems.

A key theme of the presentation was Telegram’s role in facilitating a decentralized communication infrastructure. The studies examined how official channels, military media outlets, and affiliated networks interact to disseminate messages and maintain audience engagement under conditions of conflict. According to the research, Telegram enables rapid circulation of multimedia content, real-time updates, and cross-platform amplification across broader digital networks.

The findings highlighted how messaging strategies have evolved throughout the war into increasingly sophisticated and multi-layered narratives. Dr. Amer's analysis explored the use of religious symbolism, nationalist themes, and emotional appeals within digital communications, as well as their role in identity construction, collective mobilization, and sustaining internal cohesion.

The presentation contributed to growing academic debates surrounding digital warfare, political communication, and the role of social media platforms in contemporary conflicts. By examining communication practices through a critical and interdisciplinary lens, the research offered insights into how digital technologies influence narrative competition, public perception, and information dynamics during wartime.
