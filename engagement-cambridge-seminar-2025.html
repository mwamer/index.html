Cambridge Seminar Explores the Relationship Between Academic Research and Activism Through South African and Palestinian Perspectives

17 June 2025 · University of Cambridge, United Kingdom · Panellist

Dr. Wesam Amer joined leading international scholars at the University of Cambridge for a seminar examining the intersections of research, activism, and social justice. Drawing on Palestinian experiences, he contributed to a critical discussion on the responsibilities of academics in times of conflict and the role of scholarship in confronting injustice and advancing societal change.

The University of Cambridge hosted an international seminar titled"Academic Research and Activism: Key Lessons from South Africa and Palestine," bringing together academics, researchers, students, and activists to examine the relationship between scholarly inquiry and social engagement. The event was organised by the Research for Equitable Access and Learning (REAL) Centre, the Centre for Lebanese Studies (CLS), and the Academic Action Network for Palestine (Act-for-Pal).

The discussion drew on the recently published book Research and Activism: Ruth First and Activist Research, which explores the legacy of South African scholar and anti-apartheid activist Ruth First, whose work continues to influence contemporary debates on academic responsibility and public engagement.

The seminar featured Professor Saleem Badat of the University of the Free State, Dr. Grace Idahosa of the University of Cambridge, and Dr. Wesam Amer of the University of Cambridge and Gaza University. The discussion was moderated by Dr. Maha Shuayb and Professor Yusuf Sayed.

During the event, Dr. Amer brought Palestinian perspectives to the forefront of the discussion, examining the role of academics and intellectuals in contexts marked by conflict, occupation, and educational disruption. His contribution explored how research can serve not only as a mechanism for understanding social realities but also as a means of documenting injustice, preserving knowledge, and supporting affected communities.

The seminar considered broader questions about academic freedom, public responsibility, and the relationship between scholarship and social transformation. Through comparative reflections on South Africa and Palestine, participants examined how academics have historically contributed to struggles for justice while maintaining commitments to rigorous research and intellectual integrity.

The event attracted students, researchers, and practitioners interested in the evolving role of universities in addressing contemporary global challenges. Discussions highlighted the importance of fostering connections between research and public engagement, particularly in contexts where education, knowledge production, and civic participation face significant pressures.

The seminar concluded with a call for continued dialogue on the responsibilities of academics within and beyond the university, reinforcing the importance of scholarship that contributes to both knowledge creation and broader societal understanding.
