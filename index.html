8. About / Biography

8.1. Short Biography

Dr Wesam Amer is a scholar of media, communication and political discourse whose research examines the relationships between communication, technology, conflict and power.

He is currently a CARA/IIE-SRF Fellow and Visiting Researcher at the University of Cambridge, where he conducts interdisciplinary research on digital communication, political discourse and conflict communication.

His work brings together critical discourse analysis, digital media studies and political communication to explore how political actors communicate, mobilise and construct meaning within rapidly changing media environments. His research interests include digital political communication, media and conflict, social media, artificial intelligence, platform-mediated communication, Islamist movements and emerging forms of hypermedia warfare.

Wesam has previously held academic and research positions at Gaza University, Newcastle University and Harvard University. He served as Dean of the Faculty of Mass Communication and Languages at Gaza University, was a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Fellow at Newcastle University, and a Fulbright Scholar at Harvard University's Center for Middle Eastern Studies. He completed his PhD in Media and Communication at the University of Hamburg.

8.2. Extended Academic Biography

Dr Wesam Amer is a scholar of media, communication and political discourse whose work examines how communication technologies, media systems and discursive practices shape political power, conflict and public meaning. His research is situated at the intersection of media and communication studies, digital media, political communication and critical discourse analysis, with a particular interest in the ways technological change transforms communication in contexts of conflict, geopolitical contestation and social transformation.

He is currently a CARA/IIE-SRF Fellow and Visiting Researcher at the University of Cambridge, where he conducts interdisciplinary research on digital communication, political discourse and conflict communication. His current research explores how political and non-state actors communicate within contemporary digital media ecosystems, focusing on narrative framing, visual and multimodal communication, online mobilisation and the technological conditions through which communication is distributed and made visible. He has also contributed to collaborative research on artificial intelligence, digital communication and contemporary political movements, including research supported by international funding.

Wesam's intellectual trajectory has developed through a sustained engagement with the relationship between media, conflict and political communication. His earlier work examined media representations, war reporting and political discourse, particularly in relation to the Israeli–Palestinian conflict and the wider Middle East. Over time, his research expanded to examine the transformation of political communication in digital environments, including the role of social media, online networks and new forms of mediated political participation. More recently, his work has turned increasingly towards platforms, algorithms and artificial intelligence as constitutive elements of contemporary communication rather than merely technical channels through which discourse circulates.

A central concern of his current research is the relationship between discourse and technological infrastructure. This work informs his developing approach to Techno-Discursive Critical Analysis, which seeks to connect the interpretation of language, narratives and multimodal communication with the technological systems that structure their production, circulation and visibility. Through this perspective, Wesam examines how communicative power increasingly emerges through interactions between political strategy, user practices, platform infrastructures and algorithmic systems.

His research interests include digital political communication, media and conflict, critical discourse analysis, political and strategic communication, social media, artificial intelligence, platform-mediated communication, Islamist movements and hypermedia warfare. Across these areas, he is particularly interested in understanding how political actors adapt their communication strategies to changing technological environments and how digital infrastructures influence what becomes visible, amplified, contested or marginalised in public communication.

Another important strand of Wesam's work focuses on higher education, knowledge production and institutional resilience in contexts affected by war and political violence. His recent research examines the destruction and transformation of higher education in Gaza, engaging with questions of scholasticide, academic continuity, institutional reconstruction and the future organisation of universities in post-conflict settings. This work connects his long-standing interest in communication and conflict with broader questions concerning the conditions under which knowledge institutions survive, adapt and recover.

Wesam has held academic and research positions across Palestine, the United Kingdom, Germany and the United States. He served as Dean of the Faculty of Mass Communication and Languages at Gaza University, where he combined academic leadership with teaching, curriculum development, postgraduate education and research development. His leadership experience has included institutional development, quality assurance, staff mentoring and the development of academic programmes in communication and media.

Before joining Cambridge, Wesam was a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Fellow and postdoctoral researcher at Newcastle University. He was subsequently a Fulbright Scholar and researcher at Harvard University's Center for Middle Eastern Studies. He completed his PhD in Media and Communication at the University of Hamburg, where his doctoral research contributed to his broader engagement with media, political discourse and conflict.

Alongside his research, Wesam has extensive experience in higher education teaching and postgraduate supervision. His teaching spans media and communication, digital media, media and society, research methods, visual communication and social media. His approach to teaching connects critical inquiry with contemporary communication practices, encouraging students to engage analytically with media while developing an understanding of the technological and institutional contexts in which communication is produced.

Wesam has also contributed to international academic networks and scholarly communities. He was the founder and first Chair of the Middle East Chapter of the Marie Curie Alumni Association, contributing to the development of academic connections and research networks across the region and beyond.

Through his research, teaching, academic leadership and public engagement, Wesam's work is ultimately concerned with a broader set of questions about the changing conditions of communication and knowledge. He examines how media technologies reshape political power and conflict, how digital infrastructures influence public discourse and visibility, and how institutions and knowledge communities respond to periods of profound technological and social transformation.

3. Research

3.1. Research Agenda & Overview

Overview

My research examines how communication, discourse, and technological infrastructures shape political power, conflict, legitimacy, and mobilization in contemporary societies.

Situated at the intersection of media studies, political communication, critical discourse analysis (CDA), and digital technology studies, my work investigates how states, non-state actors, platforms, and emerging technologies influence the production, circulation, and visibility of political narratives. Across different empirical contexts—particularly war, conflict, and political transformation—I explore how communication practices are embedded within broader technological, institutional, and ideological systems. By combining rigorous textual analysis with empirical investigations of platform architectures, I seek to decode how modern conflicts unfold across digital ecosystems.

Research Agenda

At its core, my research seeks to understand the evolving relationship between communication, technology, and power, driven by three overarching questions:

  • How are political realities constructed through communication? I examine how discourse, narratives, and media representations shape public understandings of conflict, legitimacy, identity, and political action.
  • How do digital and algorithmic infrastructures influence communication? My work investigates the role of platforms, algorithms, and emerging AI systems in determining visibility, engagement, and the circulation of political information.
  • How do political and non-state actors strategically communicate in conflict environments? I analyze how governments, movements, and armed non-state actors utilize communication technologies to mobilize support, govern visibility, construct legitimacy, and compete in information environments.

Grounded in both academic rigor and extensive hands-on experience as a media analyst and researcher across the Middle East, Europe, and the UK, my ongoing agenda bridges empirical digital investigations with cutting-edge theoretical frameworks in AI-mediated communication, platform governance, and international media analysis.

Research Philosophy

I view communication not merely as the transmission of information but as a contested field in which power, legitimacy, identity, and authority are continuously negotiated.

Contemporary political communication can no longer be understood exclusively through texts, narratives, or institutions. It must also account for the technological systems that shape visibility, circulation, engagement, and influence. My research therefore combines discourse-centered analysis with critical examinations of platform infrastructures, algorithms, and emerging AI systems. This perspective informs my ongoing development of Techno-Discursive Critical Analysis (TDCA) as a framework for understanding communication in technologically mediated environments.

Intellectual Trajectory

My scholarly work reflects a deliberate, continuous evolution—moving from traditional critical analysis of media war reporting toward the empirical study of algorithmic mediation, digital platforms, higher education resilience, and contemporary hypermedia warfare.

  • Phase I: War Reporting & Media Representation — Analyzing international news frameworks, structural biases, and the discursive construction of crises (e.g., Gaza conflicts, regional sieges).
  • Phase II: Digital Discourse & Political Communication — Expanding into networked public spheres, online political dynamics, and digital mobilization strategies.
  • Phase III: Social Media & Non-State Actors — Investigating how political movements and armed non-state actors (such as Hamas and Islamist movements) utilize digital platforms for strategic communication and identity building.
  • Phase IV: AI, Platforms, & Algorithmic Communication — Studying the governance of platform infrastructures, automated moderation systems, and the impact of artificial intelligence on public discourse.
  • Phase V: Scholasticide, Techno-Discursive Critical Analysis & Hypermedia Warfare — Developing pioneering theoretical frameworks that merge critical discourse studies, platform governance, security studies, and institutional recovery models (such as the study of higher education destruction in conflict zones) to map complex digital and structural conflicts.

3.2. Research Themes

Core Research Themes

My research investigates the complex intersection of digital technology, media discourse, and political power through several interconnected thematic strands:

  • Hypermedia Warfare & Conflict Communication: Investigating how contemporary conflicts increasingly unfold through interconnected ecosystems of media platforms, algorithmic systems, digital infrastructures, and AI-driven communication technologies. This strand examines the merging of physical warfare with digital information battles.
  • AI-Mediated Communication: Studying the growing influence of artificial intelligence on communication processes, information production, political discourse, algorithmic bias, and public engagement within modern digital public spheres.
  • Platform Governance & Algorithmic Visibility: Analyzing how platform architectures, automated moderation systems, recommendation algorithms, and governance frameworks dictate political visibility, attention, and the circulation of information.
  • Islamist and Non-State Actor Communication: Exploring the digital transformation, media systems, and strategic communication models of political movements and armed non-state actors (including groups such as Hamas and ISIS). This research examines how ideological actors construct legitimacy, manage online radicalization narratives, and mobilize support across shifting technological environments.
  • Digital Political Communication & Radicalisation: Examining the transformation of political communication in networked environments, focusing on online polarization, digital right-wing populism, Islamophobia, and online identity construction.
  • Higher Education in Conflict Zones (Scholasticide): Researching institutional resilience, reconstruction, and the systemic, deliberate destruction of higher education ecosystems and academic infrastructure during armed conflicts.

Regional Focus

My empirical work is anchored in critical geopolitical contexts that serve as vital laboratories for understanding broader global information shifts:

  • Middle East Wars and Politics: Analyzing the complex dynamics of regional politics, international news coverage, transitional information environments, and the role of communication technologies in shaping political power and conflict across Arab and Middle Eastern societies.
  • Palestine and Israel: Conducting deep-dive empirical investigations into the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, local and international media framing, and the specific communication strategies deployed during successive military engagements. This regional focus forms the foundation of much of my work on war reporting, state and non-state discourses, and post-conflict institutional recovery.

Flagship Initiatives & Methodological Rigor

The HYPERWAR Programme

An emerging, multi-phase research initiative investigating how contemporary conflicts unfold across interconnected digital ecosystems. This programme examines the convergence of:

  • AI-mediated communication and automated information processing.
  • Platform architectures, recommendation systems, and algorithms that dictate information flows.
  • Political and conflict communication in modern digital public spheres.
  • Hamas and other non-state actors and their evolving media systems.
  • Techno-discursive visibility and how digital prominence is weaponized or managed.
  • Hypermedia warfare as an overarching paradigm for contemporary digital conflict.

Techno-Discursive Critical Analysis (TDCA)

A distinctive theoretical and methodological framework designed to address the blind spots of traditional scholarship.

  • What TDCA Is: An interdisciplinary methodology merging Critical Discourse Studies (CDS), Critical Algorithm Studies (CAS), and Critical Security Studies (CSS) to analyze digital conflict and high-stakes information environments.
  • Why Traditional Discourse Analysis Must Evolve: Conventional textual or discourse analysis is insufficient for understanding modern political communication because it often ignores the underlying technological infrastructures that govern language, visibility, and circulation.
  • Algorithmic Power: TDCA unpacks how platform rules, recommendation algorithms, and automated moderation systems dictate what information is seen, suppressed, or amplified.
  • Applications: Deployed across current and future research initiatives to decode digital polarization, online radicalization, and state/non-state communication strategies.

Rebuilding Higher Education in Post-War Gaza

A major empirical and institutional initiative led as Principal Investigator, examining the severe impact of conflict on academic ecosystems. Key focus areas include:

  • Institutional resilience and survival strategies for academic communities in crisis zones.
  • Systemic reconstruction models for post-conflict educational recovery.
  • Digital transformation and remote learning adaptations under extreme duress.
  • Alternative frameworks for protecting higher education infrastructure and ensuring academic continuity.

Methodological Rigor & Tools

My research employs a sophisticated, multi-method approach designed to analyze complex, multimodal, and big-data digital environments by bridging qualitative critical depth with advanced computational tools:

  • Discursive Analysis:
    • Critical Discourse Analysis (CDA): Applying critical approaches to language to uncover how power relations, ideologies, and political meanings are produced, reproduced, and challenged.
    • Systemic Functional Linguistics & Socio-Semantic Inventory Models: Deconstructing specific lexical and grammatical choices to map ideological positions and social actor representations.
  • Digital & Empirical Methods:
    • Online & Digital Ethnography: Investigating online communities, digital spaces, and behavioral patterns within networked environments.
    • Social Network Analysis (SNA): Mapping information diffusion, network topologies, and the relational dynamics of online actors.
    • Multimodal & Visual Content Analysis: Analyzing the interaction of text, images, videos, and sound in digital propaganda and media framing.
  • Computational Research Tools & Software:
    • Qualitative & Mixed-Methods Analysis: NVivo for deep textual coding and thematic synthesis.
    • Network & Data Mapping: Gephi, NodeXL, 4CAT, and Voyant Tools for digital data scraping, corpus visualization, and network mapping.
    • Programming & Automation: Python for handling large-scale digital datasets and text analysis pipelines.

3.3. Research Projects

Current & Active Projects

Hamas’s Communication Strategies during the Gaza War (2023–2024)

Host Institution: Hans-Bredow-Institute and GIGA Institute (Hamburg, Germany)

Region: Middle East (Palestine, regional actors, and international reach)

Theme: Political Communication, Language, and Conflicts

Methodology: Critical Discourse Analysis, Online Ethnography, and Social Network Analysis

Timeline: 2023–Present

Abstract: This empirical and exploratory project deconstructs the digital dynamics and online discourses of Hamas amid the ongoing Gaza War (2023–2024). It examines communication outlets and social media accounts, tracks how narratives have evolved since the 2006 Palestinian legislative elections, explores how target audiences perceive and recontextualize these messages, and analyzes the broader socio-political impacts across Palestine, neighboring countries (such as Egypt and Jordan), and globally (including the USA and Germany). The research highlights a shift toward an inward-looking, self-contained media model prioritizing ideological coherence over fact-checking.

Team Members: Principal Researcher / Collaborators (Hans-Bredow-Institute and GIGA network).

Israeli Political Discourse during the Gaza War (2023–2024)

Host Institution: Al-Istiklal University (Palestine)

Region: Middle East (Israel/Palestine)

Theme: Political Communication, Language, and Conflicts

Methodology: Linguistic Analysis and Critical Discourse Analysis (CDA)

Timeline: 2023–Present

Abstract: This study employs CDA as an interpretive method to deconstruct political and media discourse in Israel during the 2023–2024 Gaza conflict. It investigates the dialectical relationship between language, discourse, and society, integrating the functional role of linguistic choices to uncover embedded political ideologies and strategic frameworks.

Team Members: Principal Researcher and institutional affiliates at Al-Istiklal University.

3.4. Intellectual Trajectory & Foundational Research

Dissertation Foundations

PhD Dissertation Project

Title: War Reporting in the International Press: A Critical Discourse Analysis of the Gaza War of 2008–2009

Host Institution: University of Hamburg

Region: Palestine and Israel

Theme: War reporting, international press coverage, and conflict discourse

Methodology: Critical Discourse Analysis (CDA)

Overview & Findings: This study analyzes the representation of social actors across four major international newspapers (The Guardian, The Times London, The New York Times, and The Washington Post). Utilizing Halliday’s transitivity model, Van Leeuwen’s socio-semantic inventory, and Richardson’s quotation patterns, the research examines how media framing and editorial policies shape the narrative of conflict. The findings reveal how international press structures agency, foreground political actors, and balance civilian suffering amidst high-stakes geopolitical reporting.

MA Dissertation Project

Title: The Coverage of the Israeli Siege of the Gaza Strip (Palestine) in the British Press

Host Institution: Anglia Ruskin University

Region: Palestine and Israel

Theme: The Israeli-Palestinian conflict, regional sieges, and press representation

Methodology: Critical Discourse Analysis and Systemic Functional Grammar

Overview & Findings: This study investigates the linguistic features and transitivity selections employed by the British press (The Guardian and The Times) in covering the blockade of the Gaza Strip. By analyzing grammatical choices, the research uncovers underlying ideological-political dimensions, the differential representation of participants as "Sensors/Sayers" versus "Actors/Attackers," and the structural exclusion or transfer of moral responsibility in international news discourse.
