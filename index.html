About

Dr. Mohammedwesam Amer

CARA/IIE-SRF Fellow

University of Cambridge

Dr. Wesam Amer is a scholar of media, communication and political discourse whose research examines the relationships between communication, technology, conflict and power. He is currently a CARA/IIE-SRF Fellow and Visiting Researcher at the University of Cambridge, where he conducts interdisciplinary research on digital communication, political discourse and conflict communication.

His work brings together critical discourse analysis, digital media studies and political communication to explore how political actors communicate, mobilise and construct meaning within rapidly changing media environments. His research interests include digital political communication, media and conflict, social media, artificial intelligence, platform-mediated communication, Islamist movements and emerging forms of hypermedia warfare.

Wesam has previously held academic and research positions at Gaza University, Newcastle University and Harvard University. He served as Dean of the Faculty of Mass Communication and Languages at Gaza University, was a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Fellow at Newcastle University, and a Fulbright Scholar at Harvard University's Center for Middle Eastern Studies. He completed his PhD in Media and Communication at the University of Hamburg.

Research Programme

Digital Political Communication

Examining how digital platforms, algorithms, and online infrastructures shape political discourse, social movements, and collective meaning-making in contemporary societies.

Media and Conflict

Analyzing how media representations, military communication, propaganda, and narrative warfare function in contemporary conflicts, with particular focus on discursive strategies and counter-narratives.

Critical Discourse Analysis

Using critical discourse analysis and multimodal methods to examine power relations, ideologies, and knowledge production embedded in communication practices.

Platform-Mediated Communication

Investigating the implications of social media platforms, algorithms, and computational systems for political discourse, content moderation, and the spread of information.

Artificial Intelligence and Communication

Exploring the implications of AI systems, large language models, and automated communication technologies for research, education, and public discourse.
