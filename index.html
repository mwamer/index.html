Research

Media, Technology, Conflict and Higher Education

My research examines how communication, technological infrastructures and institutional structures shape political power, conflict, legitimacy, knowledge production and social transformation.

Overview Agenda Pillars Frameworks Programmes Methods Regional Focus Trajectory

Research Overview

Working across media studies, political communication, critical discourse studies, digital technology studies and higher education research, I investigate two interconnected dimensions of contemporary society: how communication technologies and political actors shape the production and circulation of political narratives; and how conflict, power and institutional structures affect the production, preservation and reconstruction of knowledge.

My work brings together empirical investigations of media discourse, digital platforms and political communication with research into higher education, scholasticide, institutional resilience and academic reconstruction, particularly in Palestine and Gaza. Across these areas, I seek to understand how power operates through communication systems and institutional arrangements, and how these systems shape political and social realities.

Research Philosophy

I understand communication not simply as the transmission of information, but as a contested social and political process through which power, legitimacy, identity and authority are produced, negotiated and challenged.

Political communication cannot be fully understood through texts, narratives or media institutions alone. It also requires attention to the technological infrastructures that shape visibility, circulation, engagement and influence. Similarly, higher education cannot be understood solely through universities as physical organisations. It must also be examined through the institutional, social, technological and epistemic infrastructures that sustain academic life and knowledge production.

This perspective informs my interdisciplinary approach to both research pillars.

In media and digital technology research, I combine discourse-centred analysis with critical examinations of platform infrastructures, algorithms, digital affordances and emerging AI systems. I am particularly interested in how technological systems interact with political and ideological processes to shape the visibility and circulation of communication.

In higher education research, I examine the relationship between institutional structures, political conditions, knowledge infrastructures and academic communities. This includes investigating how universities respond to destruction and disruption, and how academic systems can be reconstructed in ways that strengthen institutional resilience and epistemic self-determination.

Across both areas, I seek to connect the analysis of discourse and technological systems with broader questions of institutional power, political agency and social transformation.

Research Agenda

My research is guided by three questions that connect the study of communication and technology with the study of institutions and knowledge production.

Question 1

How are political realities and forms of knowledge constructed through communication and institutional structures?

I examine how discourse, narratives and media representations shape public understandings of conflict, legitimacy, identity and political action. I also investigate how institutional arrangements, power relations and knowledge infrastructures influence what can be produced, preserved, recognised and communicated as knowledge.

Question 2

How do technological and institutional infrastructures shape visibility, participation and resilience?

My work investigates how platforms, algorithms and emerging AI systems influence information circulation, political visibility and engagement. In higher education, I examine how institutional infrastructures, technological resources and organisational capacities shape academic continuity, institutional survival and reconstruction, particularly under conditions of extreme disruption.

Question 3

How do political actors and institutions respond to conflict and structural transformation?

I analyse how governments, political movements and non-state actors use communication technologies to construct legitimacy, mobilise support and compete in information environments. In parallel, I investigate how universities, academics and educational communities respond to destruction, displacement and institutional crisis, and how they develop strategies for continuity, recovery and transformation.

Grounded in empirical research and interdisciplinary theoretical approaches, my agenda brings together the analysis of digital communication systems, political power, conflict environments and higher education institutions.

Research Pillars

My research is organised around two complementary pillars. Each addresses a different but interconnected dimension of power, technology and social transformation.

Pillar One

Media, Political Communication and Digital Technologies

This research examines how political actors, media organisations, digital platforms and emerging technologies shape communication, political discourse and the dynamics of contemporary conflict.

It investigates how states, political movements and non-state actors construct narratives, communicate strategically, mobilise audiences and negotiate legitimacy across traditional and digital media environments. Particular attention is given to the role of social media platforms, algorithmic systems and artificial intelligence in shaping political visibility, information circulation and public engagement.

My current work in this area explores the convergence of digital political communication, platform governance, AI-mediated communication and hypermedia warfare. It also examines how technological infrastructures influence the conditions under which political narratives are produced, amplified, restricted and contested.

Core Themes

1. Hypermedia Warfare and Conflict Communication

Investigating how contemporary conflicts unfold across interconnected ecosystems of media platforms, algorithmic systems, digital infrastructures and AI-mediated communication. This research examines the convergence of physical warfare and digital information battles, with particular attention to strategic communication, techno-discursive visibility and the circulation of conflict narratives.

2. AI-Mediated Communication and Platform Governance

Examining the growing influence of artificial intelligence, platform architectures, recommendation algorithms and automated moderation systems on political communication. This includes questions of algorithmic visibility, information governance, content moderation, digital participation and the changing conditions of public discourse.

3. Political Communication, Islamist Movements and Digital Radicalisation

Investigating how political movements and non-state actors use digital media for strategic communication, identity construction, mobilisation and the negotiation of legitimacy. This includes historical and contemporary research on Islamist movements and their media systems, as well as wider questions of online radicalisation, political polarisation, right-wing populism, Islamophobia and digital identity formation.

Research interests include:

  • Political communication and strategic communication.
  • Media representation, war reporting and conflict discourse.
  • Critical discourse analysis and digital discourse studies.
  • Social media, platforms and networked political communication.
  • AI-mediated communication and algorithmic visibility.
  • Islamist movements and non-state actor communication.
  • Digital radicalisation, political polarisation and online identities.
  • Hypermedia warfare and techno-discursive visibility.

Pillar Two

Higher Education, Knowledge and Scholasticide

This research examines how political violence, structural inequalities and institutional conditions shape higher education, knowledge production and academic life, particularly in conflict-affected societies.

A central focus is the destruction of educational institutions and knowledge infrastructures, the experiences and responses of academic communities, and the conditions required to sustain and reconstruct higher education during and after conflict.

My work engages with scholasticide and the emerging concept of de-scholasticide, examining not only the destruction of universities, academic infrastructure and educational systems, but also the political, institutional and collective processes through which higher education can be restored and transformed.

Particular attention is given to Gaza and Palestine, where the destruction of universities and the disruption of academic life raise fundamental questions about institutional survival, academic continuity, knowledge sovereignty and the future of higher education.

4. Scholasticide and the Destruction of Knowledge Infrastructures

5. De-scholasticide and Higher Education Reconstruction

This research focuses on Palestinian academic leadership, institutional recovery, human-capital regeneration, academic continuity, knowledge production and epistemic self-determination.

6. Institutional Resilience, Academic Continuity and Knowledge Production

Investigating how universities and academic communities sustain teaching, research and institutional functions under conditions of conflict, displacement and severe disruption. This includes digital transformation, remote academic collaboration, international academic solidarity and the development of resilient institutional models.

One integrated research agenda

These two pillars are connected by a broader concern with the relationship between power, communication, technology and institutions.

Political communication and digital infrastructures influence how conflicts are represented, understood and contested. At the same time, conflict transforms the institutions, communities and knowledge systems through which societies produce and preserve knowledge.

My research therefore approaches communication and higher education not as isolated domains, but as interconnected dimensions of political and social life.

Theoretical and Conceptual Frameworks

My research draws on several complementary theoretical traditions and develops conceptual approaches to address questions that cannot be adequately understood through a single disciplinary perspective.

Critical Discourse Analysis and Critical Discourse Studies

I use critical approaches to discourse to investigate how language, representation and communicative practices reproduce, legitimise and challenge relations of power. This includes the analysis of political narratives, social actor representation, ideological positioning and the discursive construction of conflict.

Critical Algorithm Studies and Platform Governance

I examine how platform architectures, algorithmic systems, recommendation mechanisms and automated moderation shape the production, circulation and visibility of information. This perspective considers platforms not merely as neutral communication channels, but as infrastructures that organise and govern digital communication.

Techno-Discursive Critical Analysis (TDCA)

Techno-Discursive Critical Analysis is a developing interdisciplinary framework through which I seek to integrate critical discourse studies with critical examinations of technological infrastructures and security-related dimensions of digital communication.

TDCA addresses a central limitation of conventional discourse-centred approaches: the tendency to analyse language and narratives without sufficiently examining the technological systems that shape their production, circulation, visibility and suppression.

The framework brings together three interconnected analytical dimensions:

  • Discursive: How language, narratives, representations and ideological positions construct political meaning.
  • Technological: How platform architectures, algorithms, automated moderation and digital infrastructures shape communication and visibility.
  • Strategic and political: How states, movements and other actors use, adapt to and contest these systems within politically sensitive and conflict-related environments.

TDCA is intended to support the systematic analysis of technologically mediated political communication, including algorithmic visibility, digital mobilisation and contemporary hypermedia warfare.

Institutional Resilience and Knowledge Reconstruction

In higher education research, I draw on institutional and organisational perspectives to examine how universities and academic communities respond to crisis, destruction and structural disruption. This includes investigating institutional resilience, academic continuity, knowledge infrastructures, reconstruction processes and the conditions necessary for the recovery of higher education systems.

Scholasticide and De-scholasticide

My work on scholasticide examines the destruction of educational institutions, academic communities and knowledge infrastructures in contexts of political violence and occupation.

My developing work on de-scholasticide considers the intellectual, institutional and practical processes required to restore higher education beyond the physical reconstruction of university buildings. It foregrounds Palestinian leadership, academic agency, knowledge continuity, institutional regeneration and epistemic self-determination.

Flagship Research Programmes

Programme 01

HYPERWAR

Developing research programme

HYPERWAR is a developing research programme investigating how contemporary conflicts unfold across interconnected digital ecosystems and how technological infrastructures shape the visibility, circulation and strategic use of political communication.

The programme brings together research on AI-mediated communication, platform governance, political communication, conflict studies and techno-discursive visibility.

Its empirical focus examines the communication systems of Hamas and Hezbollah across Telegram and TikTok, including Arabic- and English-language multimedia material, from October 2023 onwards.

The programme investigates how political and non-state actors anticipate platform environments, adapt their communication practices, respond to algorithmically mediated visibility and recalibrate their strategies in changing information environments.

Its central analytical concern is the relationship between strategic anticipation, communicative adaptation, algorithmic visibility allocation and strategic recalibration.

HYPERWAR aims to advance understanding of contemporary hypermedia warfare while contributing to interdisciplinary research on platform governance, AI-mediated communication and the regulation of digital information environments.

Programme 02

Techno-Discursive Critical Analysis (TDCA)

Developing analytical framework

TDCA is a developing theoretical and methodological framework for examining the interaction between discourse, technological infrastructures and political power.

By integrating critical discourse studies, critical algorithm studies and critical security perspectives, TDCA provides an analytical approach to examining political communication in technologically mediated and high-stakes information environments.

Its potential applications include the analysis of hypermedia warfare, digital radicalisation, algorithmic visibility, platform governance and state and non-state actor communication.

Research Methods and Tools

My research adopts an interdisciplinary, multi-method approach, combining qualitative analysis, empirical digital investigation, computational techniques and institutional research.

The choice of methods depends on the research question, empirical context and nature of the material under investigation.

Discourse and Linguistic Analysis

  • Critical Discourse Analysis (CDA): Examining how language, ideology, representation and social power interact in political and media discourse.
  • Critical Discourse Studies (CDS): Investigating the production, reproduction and contestation of political and social meanings.
  • Systemic Functional Linguistics: Analysing grammatical and linguistic choices, including transitivity, agency and representation.
  • Socio-semantic analysis: Examining how social actors are represented, classified and positioned within political and media narratives.
  • Corpus-assisted discourse analysis: Combining qualitative interpretation with systematic analysis of large textual datasets.

Digital and Computational Research

  • Social Media Research: Investigating digital communication practices, platform use and online political activity.
  • Online and Digital Ethnography: Examining digital communities, practices and interactions in networked environments.
  • Social Network Analysis: Mapping relationships, information diffusion and the network structures of digital communication.
  • Computational Text Analysis: Exploring large-scale textual datasets, patterns of language use and discursive change.
  • Multimodal and Visual Analysis: Examining the interaction of text, images, videos and sound in media and digital communication.
  • Platform and Infrastructure Analysis: Investigating platform affordances, content moderation, recommendation systems and the technological conditions shaping digital visibility.

Higher Education and Institutional Research

  • Institutional and Organisational Analysis: Examining university structures, governance, organisational responses and institutional resilience.
  • Qualitative Research: Investigating the experiences, perspectives and strategies of academics and educational communities.
  • Document and Policy Analysis: Analysing institutional policies, strategic documents and frameworks for higher education recovery.
  • Comparative and Contextual Analysis: Examining institutional challenges and reconstruction approaches across conflict-affected settings.
  • Research on Academic Continuity: Investigating digital learning, remote collaboration and alternative mechanisms for sustaining academic work during crisis.

Research Software and Computational Tools

My research draws on a range of analytical and computational tools, including:

  • NVivo: Qualitative coding, textual analysis and thematic synthesis.
  • 4CAT: Collection and analysis of digital platform data.
  • Gephi and NodeXL: Network visualisation and social network analysis.
  • Voyant Tools: Corpus exploration and text visualisation.
  • Python: Data processing, computational text analysis and research automation.

These tools support the integration of qualitative interpretation and computational investigation, while keeping the research questions and analytical framework central to the research process.

Intellectual Trajectory

My research reflects an evolving intellectual trajectory, moving from the critical analysis of international war reporting towards the empirical study of digital communication, technological infrastructures, political power and higher education in conflict-affected societies.

These phases represent the development and expansion of my research interests. They are interconnected rather than mutually exclusive, and several areas continue to inform my current work.

  1. Phase I 2008–2015

    War Reporting, Media Representation and Critical Discourse

    My early research focused on international media coverage of the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, particularly the representation of the Gaza War of 2008–2009 and the Israeli siege of the Gaza Strip.

    Through critical discourse analysis, systemic functional linguistics and socio-semantic approaches, I examined how international news organisations construct conflict narratives, represent social actors, distribute agency and frame civilian and political experiences.

    This period established the foundations of my sustained interest in media representation, political discourse and the relationship between language and power.

  2. Phase II 2015–2017

    Digital Discourse and Networked Political Communication

    Building on my earlier work in media discourse, this period marked an expansion towards digital communication environments and the changing relationship between political actors, media technologies and networked public spheres.

    My research increasingly addressed the ways political discourse and strategic communication operate beyond traditional media institutions, opening new questions about digital participation, online political expression and the transformation of public communication.

  3. Phase III 2017–2019

    Social Media, Islamist Movements and Digital Mobilisation

    During my postdoctoral research at Newcastle University, I investigated how Islamist movements and non-state actors use social media and digital communication to construct identities, circulate ideological narratives and mobilise audiences.

    This research examined online communication practices through discourse analysis, corpus linguistics, social media analysis and multimodal approaches, including the study of the Islamic State and other Islamist movements.

    It strengthened my empirical and methodological engagement with digital platforms, online radicalisation and the strategic uses of communication technologies.

    The fellowship appointment ran from 2 February 2017 to 1 February 2019, within an MSCA funding period from 1 February 2017 to 28 February 2019.

  4. Phase IV 2020–2023

    Political Communication, Institutional Leadership and Higher Education

    This period brought together my continuing research on political communication and digital media with a growing focus on higher education, institutional development and academic leadership.

    Alongside my research activities, my role as Dean of the Faculty of Mass Communication and Languages at Gaza University brought direct engagement with university governance, academic development and institutional challenges.

    My subsequent research increasingly connected questions of political conflict and communication with the vulnerability of higher education institutions and the conditions necessary to sustain academic life.

  5. Phase V 2023–Present

    AI, Platform Governance, Hypermedia Warfare and Scholasticide

    My current research brings together the two central pillars of my academic agenda.

    In media and technology research, I investigate AI-mediated communication, platform governance, algorithmic visibility and hypermedia warfare. This includes the development of Techno-Discursive Critical Analysis (TDCA) and the HYPERWAR research programme.

    In higher education research, I examine scholasticide, institutional resilience, academic continuity and the reconstruction of higher education in Gaza. This work contributes to the development of de-scholasticide as a framework for understanding academic and institutional recovery beyond physical reconstruction.

    Together, these strands explore how technological systems, political power and institutional structures shape conflict, communication and the conditions of knowledge production.

Academic Biography

A scholar between academia and the world

Dr Wesam Amer is a scholar of media, communication and higher education whose work examines how communication technologies, media systems, political discourse and institutional structures shape power, conflict and knowledge production. His interdisciplinary research connects two closely related areas: media and political communication, and higher education, knowledge and scholasticide. Across both, he is concerned with how political and institutional power operates, how societies communicate and produce knowledge under conditions of conflict and transformation, and how media and educational institutions respond to profound technological and political change.

His academic trajectory has developed through research, teaching, institutional leadership and international collaboration across Palestine, Germany, the United Kingdom and the United States. His work combines critical and interdisciplinary approaches, drawing on media and communication studies, critical discourse analysis, digital media research, political communication and higher education studies.

Media, political communication and digital technologies

A central strand of Wesam's research examines the relationship between media, political communication and power. His work investigates how political actors construct and circulate narratives, develop communication strategies, mobilise audiences and negotiate public meaning across traditional and digital media environments.

His earlier research focused on media representations, war reporting and political discourse, particularly in relation to the Israeli–Palestinian conflict and the wider Middle East. His doctoral research examined war reporting in the international press, building on his wider interest in how media institutions frame conflict, represent political actors and shape public understanding of international events.

Over time, this research expanded towards digital political communication and the transformation of political discourse through social media and networked communication. He has examined how political and non-state actors use digital platforms to communicate, mobilise supporters, construct identities and engage in strategic narrative production. His interests include Islamist movements, political and strategic communication, digital activism and the changing relationship between media, conflict and geopolitical contestation.

More recently, his research has increasingly addressed the role of platforms, algorithms and artificial intelligence in shaping contemporary communication. Rather than treating digital technologies as neutral channels, he investigates how platform infrastructures, algorithmic systems, content moderation and mechanisms of visibility influence the production, circulation and reception of political communication.

His current research explores hypermedia warfare and AI-mediated communication in contemporary conflict. It considers how political actors adapt their communicative strategies to platform environments, how visual and multimodal content circulates across digital networks, and how technological infrastructures influence the visibility and amplification of political narratives.

This work informs his developing approach to Techno-Discursive Critical Analysis (TDCA), which connects the critical interpretation of language, narratives and multimodal communication with the technological infrastructures through which discourse is produced, distributed and made visible. It provides a framework for examining the interaction between political strategy, communicative adaptation, platform governance and algorithmic visibility.

Higher education, knowledge production and scholasticide

A second major strand of Wesam's academic work concerns higher education, knowledge production and the institutional consequences of war, occupation and political violence. This research examines universities not simply as physical infrastructures or providers of education, but as essential institutions for knowledge production, cultural continuity, professional development and collective futures.

His work on Gaza focuses on the destruction of educational institutions and the wider consequences for academic communities, students, researchers and knowledge systems. It engages with the concept of scholasticide to examine the destruction of educational life and institutions, while developing a broader perspective on how higher education can be sustained, recovered and reconstructed.

An important part of this work is the development of the concept of de-scholasticide, which shifts attention from documenting destruction towards the intellectual, institutional and practical conditions required to restore and transform higher education. It considers how universities can recover beyond the reconstruction of buildings, addressing the restoration of academic communities, institutional capacity, knowledge infrastructures, research environments and educational opportunities.

His research in this area explores institutional resilience, academic continuity, human-capital regeneration, Palestinian leadership in reconstruction, epistemic self-determination and the role of international academic solidarity. It also considers how higher education institutions can move beyond emergency recovery towards more sustainable, transformative and locally grounded models of development.

Academic leadership, teaching and institutional development

Alongside his research, Wesam has extensive experience in higher education teaching, academic management and institutional development. He served as Dean of the Faculty of Mass Communication and Languages at Gaza University, where he combined academic leadership with teaching, curriculum development, postgraduate education and research development.

His academic leadership experience includes institutional planning, quality assurance, staff mentoring, programme development and the strengthening of academic and research activities.

His teaching and supervision experience spans media and communication studies, digital media, media and society, political communication, visual communication, social media and research methods. His approach connects critical academic inquiry with contemporary media practices and encourages students to understand communication within its wider technological, political and institutional contexts.

International research experience and academic collaborations

Wesam's academic career includes research and academic appointments in several international institutions.

He was a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Postdoctoral Fellow at Newcastle University (2017–2019), where he developed his research through an internationally funded research fellowship. In 2022, he was a Fulbright Research Scholar at Harvard University's Center for Middle Eastern Studies, undertaking research on Hamas and digital communication.

Since December 2023, he has been a guest researcher at the Hans-Bredow-Institut in Hamburg. He is also a Visiting Scholar at Jesus College, University of Cambridge.

His international research experience has enabled him to develop collaborations across academic institutions and disciplinary communities, contributing to wider discussions on media, political communication, conflict, digital technologies and higher education.

He was also the founder and first Chair of the Middle East Chapter of the Marie Curie Alumni Association, contributing to the development of academic networks and research connections across the region and internationally.

An integrated research agenda

Although media and political communication and higher education may appear to be distinct academic fields, they are connected by a common intellectual concern: understanding how power, technology, conflict and institutional structures shape the conditions under which societies communicate, produce knowledge and organise their futures.

In the media sector, his research examines how political actors communicate, how digital infrastructures shape public discourse, and how technological systems influence visibility, influence and power.

In the education sector, his research examines how knowledge institutions are affected by conflict and political violence, how academic communities maintain intellectual and professional continuity, and how higher education can be reconstructed in ways that restore institutional capacity and strengthen epistemic autonomy.

Together, these areas form an integrated academic and professional agenda connecting communication, political power, technology, education and knowledge transformation.
