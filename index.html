8. About / Biography

8.1. Short Biography

Dr Wesam Amer is a scholar of media, communication and political discourse whose research examines the relationships between communication, technology, conflict and power.

He is currently a CARA/IIE-SRF Fellow and Visiting Researcher at the University of Cambridge, where he conducts interdisciplinary research on digital communication, political discourse and conflict communication.

His work brings together critical discourse analysis, digital media studies and political communication to explore how political actors communicate, mobilise and construct meaning within rapidly changing media environments. His research interests include digital political communication, media and conflict, social media, artificial intelligence, platform-mediated communication, Islamist movements and emerging forms of hypermedia warfare.

Wesam has previously held academic and research positions at Gaza University, Newcastle University and Harvard University. He served as Dean of the Faculty of Mass Communication and Languages at Gaza University, was a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Fellow at Newcastle University, and a Fulbright Scholar at Harvard University's Center for Middle Eastern Studies. He completed his PhD in Media and Communication at the University of Hamburg.

8.2. Extended Academic Biography

Dr Wesam Amer is a scholar of media, communication and political discourse whose work examines how communication technologies, media systems and discursive practices shape political power, conflict and public meaning. His research is situated at the intersection of media and communication studies, digital media, political communication and critical discourse analysis, with a particular interest in the ways technological change transforms communication in contexts of conflict, geopolitical contestation and social transformation.

He is currently a CARA/IIE-SRF Fellow and Visiting Researcher at the University of Cambridge, where he conducts interdisciplinary research on digital communication, political discourse and conflict communication. His current research explores how political and non-state actors communicate within contemporary digital media ecosystems, focusing on narrative framing, visual and multimodal communication, online mobilisation and the technological conditions through which communication is distributed and made visible. He has also contributed to collaborative research on artificial intelligence, digital communication and contemporary political movements, including research supported by international funding.

Wesam's intellectual trajectory has developed through a sustained engagement with the relationship between media, conflict and political communication. His earlier work examined media representations, war reporting and political discourse, particularly in relation to the Israeli–Palestinian conflict and the wider Middle East. Over time, his research expanded to examine the transformation of political communication in digital environments, including the role of social media, online networks and new forms of mediated political participation. More recently, his work has turned increasingly towards platforms, algorithms and artificial intelligence as constitutive elements of contemporary communication rather than merely technical channels through which discourse circulates.

A central concern of his current research is the relationship between discourse and technological infrastructure. This work informs his developing approach to Techno-Discursive Critical Analysis, which seeks to connect the interpretation of language, narratives and multimodal communication with the technological systems that structure their production, circulation and visibility. Through this perspective, Wesam examines how communicative power increasingly emerges through interactions between political strategy, user practices, platform infrastructures and algorithmic systems.

His research interests include digital political communication, media and conflict, critical discourse analysis, political and strategic communication, social media, artificial intelligence, platform-mediated communication, Islamist movements and hypermedia warfare. Across these areas, he is particularly interested in understanding how political actors adapt their communication strategies to changing technological environments and how digital infrastructures influence what becomes visible, amplified, contested or marginalised in public communication.

Another important strand of Wesam's work focuses on higher education, knowledge production and institutional resilience in contexts affected by war and political violence. His recent research examines the destruction and transformation of higher education in Gaza, engaging with questions of scholasticide, academic continuity, institutional reconstruction and the future organisation of universities in post-conflict settings. This work connects his long-standing interest in communication and conflict with broader questions concerning the conditions under which knowledge institutions survive, adapt and recover.

Wesam has held academic and research positions across Palestine, the United Kingdom, Germany and the United States. He served as Dean of the Faculty of Mass Communication and Languages at Gaza University, where he combined academic leadership with teaching, curriculum development, postgraduate education and research development. His leadership experience has included institutional development, quality assurance, staff mentoring and the development of academic programmes in communication and media.

Before joining Cambridge, Wesam was a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Fellow and postdoctoral researcher at Newcastle University. He was subsequently a Fulbright Scholar and researcher at Harvard University's Center for Middle Eastern Studies. He completed his PhD in Media and Communication at the University of Hamburg, where his doctoral research contributed to his broader engagement with media, political discourse and conflict.

Alongside his research, Wesam has extensive experience in higher education teaching and postgraduate supervision. His teaching spans media and communication, digital media, media and society, research methods, visual communication and social media. His approach to teaching connects critical inquiry with contemporary communication practices, encouraging students to engage analytically with media while developing an understanding of the technological and institutional contexts in which communication is produced.

Wesam has also contributed to international academic networks and scholarly communities. He was the founder and first Chair of the Middle East Chapter of the Marie Curie Alumni Association, contributing to the development of academic connections and research networks across the region and beyond.

Through his research, teaching, academic leadership and public engagement, Wesam's work is ultimately concerned with a broader set of questions about the changing conditions of communication and knowledge. He examines how media technologies reshape political power and conflict, how digital infrastructures influence public discourse and visibility, and how institutions and knowledge communities respond to periods of profound technological and social transformation.
