Teaching approach

I regard teaching as an intellectual and collaborative process through which students develop the knowledge, confidence and independence to engage critically with their disciplines and the wider world.

  • 01
    Research-led teaching

    I connect teaching with current scholarship and my own research, so that students engage with academic debates, assess evidence and see how knowledge is produced and contested.

  • 02
    Critical and independent thinking

    I encourage students to question assumptions, evaluate arguments and examine the relationship between media, technology, politics and society, developing analytical judgement rather than relying on the transmission of information.

  • 03
    Theory connected to practice

    I bring conceptual frameworks into dialogue with contemporary cases and professional practice, so students see the relevance of theory and build practical analytical skills.

  • 04
    Inclusive and responsive learning

    Students bring different educational experiences and perspectives. I adapt methods to academic level and learning context, so that every student can participate and develop.

  • 05
    Intellectual and professional development

    I aim to build transferable capacities: research, critical analysis, academic writing, clear communication and collaboration in changing professional and technological environments.

  • 06
    Digital, AI and technology-enhanced teaching

    I help students use AI and digital tools effectively, responsibly and transparently, with attention to authorship, evidence, reliability, bias and academic integrity. Digital technologies are both teaching resources and subjects of critical inquiry.

Having taught in Palestinian universities, I also pay attention to continuity and quality of learning in difficult conditions, a concern that connects to my research on higher education and reconstruction.

Supervision, examination and mentorship

My supervision supports students in developing independent research projects, strengthening their methods and producing rigorous academic work. At Cambridge, my contribution includes undergraduate and postgraduate supervision and examination.

I see supervision as more than completing a dissertation: it supports students' development as independent researchers, critical thinkers and emerging academic or professional practitioners.

  • Undergraduate
    I help students develop research questions, find relevant literature, choose appropriate methods and build coherent academic arguments, with growing confidence in their own research decisions.
  • Master's
    Postgraduate supervision focuses on research design, theoretical frameworks, literature reviews, methodological choices, data collection and analysis, and a clear and original contribution.
  • Doctoral research advice
    I advise doctoral researchers working in political communication, media and discourse, digital technologies, conflict communication and higher education, on conceptual framing, research design, methodological development and the interpretation of findings.
  • Examination and assessment
    I assess the coherence of research questions, theoretical understanding, methodological rigour, quality of analysis and contribution to knowledge.
