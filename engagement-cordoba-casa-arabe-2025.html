Dr. Wesam Amer Addresses International Conference in Córdoba on Media Narratives and Information Integrity During the Gaza War

9 April 2025 · Casa Árabe, Córdoba, Spain · Invited Speaker

Dr. Wesam Amer delivered a featured lecture in Córdoba, Spain, examining the role of international media in shaping perceptions of the Gaza war. Drawing on his expertise in communication and conflict studies, he highlighted the consequences of biased reporting, the importance of ethical journalism, and the responsibility of scholars to challenge misinformation and strengthen public understanding.

Dr. Wesam Amer participated in a major academic event in Córdoba, Spain, where he examined the role of international media in shaping public perceptions of the Gaza war and discussed broader challenges relating to information integrity, media ethics, and public discourse. The event was hosted by Casa Árabe in collaboration with the University of Córdoba as part of its Aula Árabe Universitaria programme.

During his presentation, titled “Insights from International Media Coverage of the Israeli War on Gaza 2023–2025: Public Opinion Fabrication and Western Audience Engineering,” Dr. Amer offered a critical analysis of how media narratives influence public understanding of conflict. Drawing on comparative research examining media coverage in the United States, the United Kingdom, and Germany, he explored patterns of framing, narrative construction, and information dissemination in times of war.

Central to his lecture was the importance of ethical journalism and the need for rigorous reporting standards in conflict environments. Dr. Amer discussed how terminology, sourcing practices, and narrative framing can significantly affect audiences' interpretations of events. He also highlighted the responsibilities of scholars and researchers in critically examining media narratives, challenging misinformation, and contributing evidence-based analysis to public debate.

The event generated significant interest among academics, students, journalists, and members of the public, prompting extensive discussion about media accountability and the role of communication in contemporary conflicts. According to organisers, the lecture formed part of broader efforts to encourage critical reflection on the influence of news media in shaping international perspectives on the Gaza war.

Dr. Amer's participation reinforced growing international discussions concerning media literacy, journalistic responsibility, and the challenges of reporting on highly contested conflicts. The event concluded with a call for continued dialogue on professional ethics, evidence-based reporting, and the importance of diverse perspectives in global media coverage.
