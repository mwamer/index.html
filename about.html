About Dr Wesam Amer

Scholar · Expert · Consultant

Media, AI, Conflict & Higher Education

Dr Wesam Amer is an academic, researcher and consultant specialising in political communication, media and digital technologies, artificial intelligence, and higher education in conflict-affected contexts. His work examines how communication, technology and political power shape public understanding, political narratives and the production and circulation of knowledge.

With an academic career spanning Palestine, Germany, the United Kingdom and the United States, he brings together interdisciplinary research, university teaching, institutional leadership and professional engagement. His work connects critical scholarship with contemporary challenges facing media, political institutions and higher education, with particular attention to Palestine and the wider Middle East.

He is currently an Affiliated Lecturer at Cambridge Digital Humanities, University of Cambridge, and the Founder and Director of Frontline World and the Gaza Center for Research in Humanities and Social Sciences.

Academic and Intellectual Profile

His academic work is driven by an interest in the relationship between communication, technology and power, and in the ways conflict transforms the institutions and systems through which societies produce and share knowledge.

Across his research and teaching, three closely connected areas form the foundation of his work:

Political Communication, Media and Conflict

He investigates how political actors, movements and media institutions construct narratives, communicate political objectives and shape public understanding during conflict and political change. His work draws on critical discourse studies and political communication to examine media representation, digital political expression and the relationship between communication and power.

Artificial Intelligence, Digital Technologies and Algorithmic Power

He examines how digital platforms, artificial intelligence and algorithmic systems transform communication, media production and the visibility of information. His research considers how political actors adapt to platform environments and how technological infrastructures influence the circulation and interpretation of digital content.

Higher Education, Scholasticide and Reconstruction

He researches the destruction and disruption of educational institutions in conflict, particularly in Palestine, and the conditions required to restore academic communities and knowledge production. This work moves beyond physical reconstruction to consider institutional capacity, academic continuity, governance and the recovery of higher education systems.

Communication, power and knowledge under conflict.

Together, these areas reflect a broader intellectual concern: Who can speak, be seen and produce knowledge under conditions of conflict and technological change?

Research and Knowledge Production

Dr Amer's research combines critical theoretical inquiry with empirical and interdisciplinary methods. His work draws on Critical Discourse Studies, political communication, media studies, critical algorithm studies, critical security studies and higher education research.

He is developing two conceptual contributions that connect his research agendas.

Techno-Discursive Critical Analysis (TDCA)

Brings together critical discourse studies, critical algorithm studies and critical security studies to examine how political discourse interacts with technological infrastructures, algorithmic systems and the allocation of visibility in digital environments.

De-scholasticide

Extends the analysis of scholasticide by examining what is required to restore educational communities, institutional capacity and knowledge production following the destruction of higher education systems.

HYPERWAR

Examines AI-mediated hypermedia warfare and algorithmic visibility in contemporary conflict, and research on the reconstruction of Palestinian higher education.

His methodological experience includes critical discourse analysis, systemic functional linguistics, corpus-assisted research, social media and digital trace analysis, multimodal analysis, social network analysis and institutional case studies. He works with qualitative, computational and interpretive approaches to investigate complex communication and institutional processes.

Teaching and Academic Development

Teaching is an integral part of Dr Amer's academic work. He has contributed to undergraduate and postgraduate education across Palestine, the United Kingdom and Germany, as well as guest lecturing at Harvard University during his Fulbright Fellowship.

His teaching covers media and communication, journalism, digital media, political communication, international affairs and research methods. He connects academic theory with contemporary developments and encourages students to question assumptions, evaluate evidence, develop independent arguments and undertake rigorous research.

His teaching approach is research-led, interdisciplinary and attentive to the different educational backgrounds and experiences of students. He is particularly interested in the implications of digital technologies and generative AI for teaching, academic work, research practices and educational integrity.

At the University of Cambridge, his academic contribution includes lecturing, undergraduate and postgraduate supervision, and examination. His wider academic interests include research mentorship, curriculum development and the future of higher education in challenging contexts.

Academic Journey and Institutional Experience

Dr Amer's academic journey began in Palestine, where he studied mass communication before pursuing postgraduate studies in the United Kingdom and Germany.

He holds a PhD in Media Studies and Discourse Analysis from the University of Hamburg, awarded Magna Cum Laude in 2015. His doctoral research examined international press coverage of the 2008–09 Gaza war through Critical Discourse Analysis. He also holds an MA with Distinction from Anglia Ruskin University and a BA from Al-Azhar University in Gaza.
