About

Dr Wesam Amer

Scholar · Expert · Consultant in media, AI, conflict and higher education

Dr Wesam Amer is an academic, researcher and consultant specialising in political communication, media and digital technologies, artificial intelligence, and higher education in conflict-affected contexts.

His work examines how communication, technology and political power shape public understanding, political narratives and the production and circulation of knowledge, with particular attention to Palestine and the wider Middle East.

Profile

A career across Palestine, Germany, the UK and the US

He is Affiliated Lecturer at Cambridge Digital Humanities, University of Cambridge, and Founder and Director of Frontline World and of the Gaza Center for Research in Humanities and Social Sciences (GCR-HSS).

His career spans Palestine, Germany, the United Kingdom and the United States, and brings together research, university teaching, institutional leadership and professional engagement.

Research

What drives his work

Three connected areas of research share one question: who can speak, be seen and produce knowledge under conditions of conflict and technological change?

I

Political communication, media and conflict

How political actors, movements and media institutions construct narratives and shape public understanding.

II

AI, digital technologies and algorithmic power

How platforms and algorithmic systems transform communication and the visibility of information.

III

Higher education, scholasticide and reconstruction

The destruction of educational institutions in conflict, particularly in Palestine, and the conditions for restoring academic communities and knowledge production.

He is developing two frameworks that link these areas: Techno-Discursive Critical Analysis (TDCA) and de-scholasticide. Current projects include HYPERWAR, on AI-mediated hypermedia warfare, and research on rebuilding Palestinian higher education.

Teaching

Research-led and interdisciplinary

He has taught undergraduate and postgraduate students in Palestine, the United Kingdom and Germany, and guest lectured at Harvard University as a Fulbright Fellow. At Cambridge he lectures, supervises and examines. His teaching is research-led and interdisciplinary, with growing attention to digital technologies and generative AI in academic work.

Leadership

Institutional leadership and scholarly service

From 2020 to 2023 he was Dean of the Faculty of Mass Communication and Languages at Gaza University. He is also Founder and first Chair of the Middle East Chapter of the Marie Curie Alumni Association.
