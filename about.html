About

Dr Wesam Amer

Scholar of Media, Political Communication and Higher Education

Dr Wesam Amer is a scholar of media, political communication and higher education whose interdisciplinary research examines how communication technologies, political discourse and institutional structures shape power, conflict and knowledge production.

His work spans Palestine, Germany, the United Kingdom and the United States, connecting academic research with teaching, institutional leadership and professional engagement. He is currently an Affiliated Lecturer at Cambridge Digital Humanities, University of Cambridge, and Founder and Director of Frontline World and the Gaza Center for Research in Humanities and Social Sciences.

Research Focus

His work is organised around three interconnected areas that share a common concern with communication, power and knowledge under conditions of conflict and technological change.

Area 1

Political Communication, Media and Conflict

Examining how political actors and media institutions construct narratives, communicate political objectives and shape public understanding during conflict and political change, drawing on critical discourse studies and political communication.

Area 2

Artificial Intelligence, Digital Technologies and Algorithmic Power

Investigating how digital platforms, AI and algorithmic systems transform communication, media production and visibility, and how technological infrastructures shape the circulation of political content.

Area 3

Higher Education, Scholasticide and Reconstruction

Researching the destruction of educational institutions in conflict, particularly Palestine, and the conditions required to restore academic communities, institutional capacity and knowledge production.

Central concern: Who can speak, be seen and produce knowledge under conditions of conflict and technological change?

Academic Biography

Dr Amer holds a PhD in Media Studies and Discourse Analysis from the University of Hamburg (2015, Magna Cum Laude), an MA with Distinction from Anglia Ruskin University (2009), and a BA from Al-Azhar University in Gaza (2001). His doctoral research examined international press coverage of the 2008-09 Gaza war through Critical Discourse Analysis.

His academic appointments include Marie Skłodowska-Curie Fellow at Newcastle University (2017-2020), Fulbright Fellow at Harvard University (2022), Guest Researcher at the Hans-Bredow-Institut (2023-2024), CARA/IIE-SRF Fellow at the University of Cambridge (2024-2026), and Affiliated Lecturer at Cambridge Digital Humanities (2026-2027). He also held visiting positions at Jesus College, Homerton College and the Centre for Islamic Studies at the University of Cambridge. From 2020 to 2023, he served as Dean of the Faculty of Mass Communication and Languages at Gaza University.

He is developing two conceptual contributions: Techno-Discursive Critical Analysis (TDCA), which connects critical discourse studies with critical algorithm studies and critical security studies to examine discourse through technological infrastructures; and de-scholasticide, which focuses on restoring and transforming higher education following institutional destruction. His current research includes HYPERWAR, examining AI-mediated hypermedia warfare and algorithmic visibility in contemporary conflict.

Alongside research and teaching, he is Founder and Director of Frontline World and the Gaza Center for Research in Humanities and Social Sciences. He works in Arabic, English and German and is a member of the Marie Curie Alumni Association (founding Chair of its Middle East Chapter).
