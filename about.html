Academic Profile

Dr Wesam Amer

Scholar Researcher Consultant
Who can speak, be seen and produce knowledge under conditions of conflict and technological change?

Dr Wesam Amer is an academic, researcher and consultant specialising in political communication, media and digital technologies, artificial intelligence, and higher education in conflict-affected contexts. His work examines how communication, technology and political power shape public understanding, political narratives and the production and circulation of knowledge.

With an academic career spanning Palestine, Germany, the United Kingdom and the United States, he brings together interdisciplinary research, university teaching, institutional leadership and professional engagement. Currently an Affiliated Lecturer at Cambridge Digital Humanities, University of Cambridge, and Founder and Director of Frontline World and the Gaza Center for Research in Humanities and Social Sciences.

Core Focus Areas

Intellectual Pillars

Three closely connected areas form the foundation of his work, united by a common concern with communication, power and knowledge under conditions of conflict and technological change.

01

Political Communication, Media and Conflict

Investigating how political actors, movements and media institutions construct narratives, communicate objectives and shape public understanding during conflict and political change through critical discourse studies and political communication.

02

Artificial Intelligence, Digital Technologies and Algorithmic Power

Examining how digital platforms, artificial intelligence and algorithmic systems transform communication, media production and information visibility, and how technological infrastructures influence content circulation and interpretation.

03

Higher Education, Scholasticide and Reconstruction

Researching the destruction and disruption of educational institutions in conflict, particularly Palestine, and the conditions required to restore academic communities, institutional capacity, governance and knowledge production beyond physical reconstruction.

Theoretical Contributions

Conceptual Frameworks & Methods

Developing innovative approaches that bridge critical theory with technological analysis to understand discourse in digital environments.

1

Techno-Discursive Critical Analysis (TDCA)

Brings together critical discourse studies, critical algorithm studies and critical security studies to examine how political discourse interacts with technological infrastructures, algorithmic systems and the allocation of visibility in digital environments.

2

De-scholasticide

Extends the analysis of scholasticide by examining what is required to restore educational communities, institutional capacity and knowledge production following the destruction of higher education systems, moving beyond physical reconstruction.

3

HYPERWAR

Current research examining AI-mediated hypermedia warfare and algorithmic visibility in contemporary conflict, analyzing how political actors adapt communicative strategies to platform environments and how technological infrastructures influence narrative circulation.
