Dr. Wesam Amer Discusses Media Coverage of the Gaza War in Interview with elDiario.es

April 2025 · elDiario.es, Spain · Interviewee

Dr. Wesam Amer, Dean of the Faculty of Communication and Languages at Gaza University and a researcher at the University of Cambridge, spoke with elDiario.es about his research into international media coverage of the Gaza war. Drawing on comparative analysis of German and English-language media, he examined reporting patterns, source selection, narrative framing, and their influence on public perception.

Dr. Wesam Amer has contributed to the ongoing international debate surrounding media coverage of the Gaza war through an interview with the Spanish news platform elDiario.es, where he discussed findings from his research on the representation of the conflict in Western media. The interview focused on how narratives are constructed, how sources are selected, and how public understanding of conflict is shaped through news reporting.

As Dean of the Faculty of Communication and Languages at Gaza University and a visiting researcher at the University of Cambridge, Dr. Amer has been examining media coverage of the war since October 2023. His research analyzes reporting patterns in both German and English-language media outlets, with particular attention to framing strategies, source dependence, and the presentation of competing narratives.

During the interview, Dr. Amer discussed what he identified as recurring tendencies in Western media coverage, arguing that reporting frequently relies on Israeli official sources and narratives while Palestinian perspectives receive less visibility. He explained that such patterns influence how international audiences interpret events and understand the broader political and historical context of the conflict.

The interview also addressed wider questions concerning journalism ethics, information verification, and the responsibilities of media organizations during periods of war. Drawing on communication and media studies methodologies, Dr. Amer emphasized the importance of critical engagement with information sources and highlighted the role of scholars in analyzing how narratives are formed and circulated.

A central theme of the discussion was the relationship between media representation and public opinion. Dr. Amer argued that the framing of conflicts has long-term implications for international awareness, political debate, and public engagement. His research seeks to contribute to broader conversations about media accountability, communication in conflict environments, and the role of journalism in informing public understanding.

The interview forms part of a growing body of international scholarly work examining media narratives, conflict reporting, and the challenges facing journalism in highly contested political contexts. Through his contributions, Dr. Amer continues to engage academic, media, and public audiences in discussions about communication, conflict, and the ethics of reporting.
