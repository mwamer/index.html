RESEARCH

Media, Technology, Political Power & Higher Education

Dr Wesam Amer’s research examines the relationship between communication, technology, political power and the production of knowledge. His work brings together political communication, Critical Discourse Studies, digital media research, critical approaches to artificial intelligence and research on higher education in conflict-affected contexts.

His research explores how political actors and media institutions construct narratives, how digital platforms and algorithmic systems shape communication and visibility, and how conflict disrupts educational institutions and academic communities.

Who can speak, be seen and produce knowledge under conditions of conflict and technological change?

Three interconnected research agendas. Two developing intellectual contributions. A shared interest in communication, power, conflict and knowledge production.

Three Interconnected Research Agendas

Dr Amer’s research is organised around three interconnected areas of inquiry. Each addresses a different dimension of the relationship between communication, technology, political power and knowledge production.

Political Communication, Media & Conflict

Examines how political actors, movements and media institutions construct narratives, communicate political objectives and shape public understanding during conflict and political change.

  • Media representation and political narratives
  • Conflict communication and political legitimacy
  • Digital communication by political and non-state actors
  • Islamist movements and mediated political expression
  • Media discourse, security narratives and political power

AI, Digital Technologies & Algorithmic Power

Examines how digital platforms, artificial intelligence and algorithmic systems transform political communication, media production and the circulation and visibility of digital content.

  • AI-mediated political communication
  • Platform governance and algorithmic visibility
  • AI-generated, AI-assisted and digitally manipulated media
  • Multimodal communication across social media platforms
  • Political actors’ adaptation to changing technological environments
  • Digital infrastructures, security and political power

Higher Education, Scholasticide & Reconstruction

Examines the destruction and disruption of higher education, particularly in Palestine, and the institutional, human and organisational conditions necessary to restore academic communities and knowledge production.

  • Scholasticide and the destruction of educational institutions
  • Academic continuity under war and occupation
  • Institutional governance and organisational resilience
  • University reconstruction and recovery
  • Academic communities and knowledge production
  • The future of higher education in Palestine

Developing Intellectual Contributions

Two developing contributions extend Dr Amer’s research in complementary directions. TDCA connects the study of political communication with technological infrastructures and algorithmic systems. De-scholasticide develops a conceptual approach to the restoration of academic communities and knowledge production following the destruction of higher education.

Techno-Discursive Critical Analysis

A developing analytical framework

Techno-Discursive Critical Analysis (TDCA) brings together Critical Discourse Studies, Critical Algorithm Studies and Critical Security Studies. It examines how political discourse interacts with technological infrastructures, platform environments and algorithmic systems.

Rather than treating digital communication as language or content alone, TDCA considers how communicative practices are shaped by the technological conditions through which content is produced, circulated and made visible.

De-scholasticide

A developing conceptual approach

De-scholasticide extends the discussion of scholasticide beyond the destruction of educational institutions. It asks what is required to restore the academic communities, institutional capacities and conditions of knowledge production that have been damaged or dismantled.

The approach places emphasis on academic continuity, institutional governance, human and organisational capacity, resilience and the restoration of higher education ecosystems.

Current and Developing Research Projects

These projects demonstrate how Dr Amer’s research agendas are pursued through empirical investigation, conceptual development and interdisciplinary research.

Featured Project

AI-Mediated Hypermedia Warfare in Contemporary Conflict

Ongoing research project

HYPERWAR examines AI-mediated communication and algorithmic visibility in contemporary conflict. It focuses on Hamas and Hezbollah, and on communication across Telegram and TikTok.

The project investigates how political actors anticipate and adapt to changing platform environments, how multimodal and AI-mediated content circulates, and how technological systems shape the visibility of conflict-related communication.

Actors: Hamas and Hezbollah | Platforms: Telegram and TikTok | Focus: AI-mediated content, multimodal communication, technological infrastructures and algorithmic visibility | Approach: Techno-Discursive Critical Analysis (TDCA)

Research Programme

Hamas Digital Communication

A body of research examining Hamas’s digital communication, political narratives and multimodal practices. Considers how digital communication constructs political meanings and communicates with different audiences, providing foundation for HYPERWAR.

Ongoing Research Agenda

De-scholasticide and the Reconstruction of Higher Education in Gaza

Exploring how higher education can be restored following the destruction and disruption of universities and academic communities in Gaza. Develops the concept of de-scholasticide and examines institutional, organisational and human conditions necessary for academic continuity and reconstruction.

Ongoing Research

Rebuilding Palestinian Higher Education

Research on the institutional and organisational rebuilding of Palestinian higher education. Considers university governance, institutional capacity, organisational resilience and conditions for sustainable recovery.

Theoretical Foundations

Dr Amer’s research draws on several complementary fields. These perspectives are brought together according to the research question rather than applied as a single, fixed theoretical model.

Critical Discourse Studies

Discourse, representation, ideology, power and the production of meaning.

Political Communication and Media Studies

Political narratives, media systems, public communication and mediated conflict.

Critical Algorithm Studies and Platform Governance

Algorithmic systems, platform infrastructures, visibility and the governance of digital communication.

Critical Security and Conflict Studies

Security narratives, political violence, conflict and the relationship between technology and power.

Higher Education and Institutional Studies

University governance, institutional capacity, resilience and the organisation of knowledge production.
