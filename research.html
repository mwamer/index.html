Research

Who can speak, be seen and know under conflict

My research examines how conflict, technology and political power shape the production, circulation and loss of knowledge. It follows two connected agendas. The first studies how media and AI-mediated systems construct political narratives and govern what becomes visible in conflict. The second studies how war destroys, and how societies rebuild, the universities that sustain knowledge production, particularly in Palestine.

Both ask who can speak, be seen and know under violence. I develop a framework for each: Techno-Discursive Critical Analysis (TDCA) and de-scholasticide, the study of how education is restored after scholasticide, its systematic destruction.

Research

Research pillars and questions

I

Pillar I: Political Communication, Media and Conflict

I investigate how political actors, movements and media institutions construct narratives, communicate political objectives and shape public understanding in contexts of conflict and insecurity.

Guiding questions

  • How do media representations shape understandings of conflict, legitimacy and power?
  • How do Islamist movements use digital communication for political mobilisation and strategic communication?
  • How are terrorism, extremism and security framed in political and media discourse?

Evidence: PhD research on Gaza war coverage · Hamas in Cyberspace (2023) · Critical Multimodal Discourse Analysis (2024)

II

Pillar II: Artificial Intelligence, Digital Technologies and Algorithmic Power

I examine how artificial intelligence, digital platforms and algorithmic systems transform communication, political influence and the visibility of information. This pillar asks how political actors adapt to algorithmically mediated environments, and what AI-generated, manipulated and multimodal media mean for conflict and security.

Guiding questions

  • How does AI transform political communication and the production of media content?
  • How do platform infrastructures and algorithms shape the visibility and circulation of political information?
  • What role do AI-generated, manipulated and multimodal media play in contemporary conflict and information warfare?

Evidence: HYPERWAR: AI-Mediated Hypermedia Warfare · TDCA framework

III

Pillar III: Higher Education, Scholasticide and Reconstruction

I investigate the destruction and disruption of higher education in conflict-affected societies, with particular attention to Palestine. Scholasticide, the systematic destruction of education, damages universities, academic communities and the continuity of knowledge production. My work asks what reconstruction requires beyond rebuilt infrastructure, including governance, institutional resilience and academic capacity.

Guiding questions

  • How does the destruction of educational institutions affect academic communities and knowledge production?
  • How can universities maintain academic continuity under war and occupation?
  • What does de-scholasticide require beyond the physical reconstruction of educational infrastructure?

Evidence: Beyond Scholasticide: De-Scholasticide and the Reconstruction of Higher Education in Gaza · Rebuilding Higher Education in Post-War Gaza · Re-engineering Palestinian Universities

Conceptual contributions

My research engages established theoretical traditions while developing analytical approaches to emerging questions at the intersection of communication, technology, conflict and knowledge production.

Developing framework

Techno-Discursive Critical Analysis (TDCA)

TDCA integrates Critical Discourse Studies, Critical Algorithm Studies and Critical Security Studies. It asks how political discourse interacts with technological infrastructures, algorithmic systems and the distribution of visibility in digital information environments. Rather than treating language and technology separately, it examines how narratives, platform architectures and algorithmic ranking together shape what becomes visible and legitimate in conflict. TDCA links Pillars I and II and is applied in the HYPERWAR project.

Research application: HYPERWAR: AI-Mediated Hypermedia Warfare
Developing conceptual approach

De-scholasticide

De-scholasticide moves beyond documenting the destruction of educational institutions to examine the processes and conditions needed to restore academic communities, institutional capacity and knowledge production. Where scholasticide names the destruction, de-scholasticide asks what recovery requires: academic continuity, governance, institutional resilience and the rebuilding of the human and organisational capacity of universities. It belongs primarily to Pillar III and is applied to Gaza and to Palestinian higher education more widely.

Research application: Beyond Scholasticide · Rebuilding Higher Education in Post-War Gaza

Theoretical foundations and methods

My work draws on interdisciplinary traditions and uses qualitative, interpretive, computational and comparative methods, chosen according to the research question and the empirical material.

Theoretical foundations

  • Critical Discourse Studies and Systemic Functional Linguistics: language, representation, ideology and the construction of social actors.
  • Political Communication and Media Studies: framing, propaganda, political narratives and media power.
  • Critical Algorithm Studies and Platform Governance: algorithmic mediation, platform infrastructures and information visibility.
  • Critical Security and Conflict Studies: security narratives, political violence, terrorism, extremism and conflict communication.
  • Higher Education and Institutional Studies: institutional governance, academic resilience, knowledge production and organisational reconstruction.

Methods and applications

Discourse and communication analysis
Critical Discourse Analysis, discourse-historical analysis, Systemic Functional Linguistics, framing and narrative analysis
Digital and computational research
Social media analysis, digital trace analysis, corpus analysis, metadata analysis, digital ethnography
Multimodal research
Analysis of textual, visual, audiovisual and AI-mediated content
Network analysis
Social network analysis, digital mapping and visualisation
Institutional and comparative research
Case studies, document analysis, institutional assessment, comparative analysis

HYPERWAR

Examines how political communication, AI-mediated content, platform infrastructures and algorithmic visibility interact in contemporary conflict, focusing on Hamas and Hezbollah across Telegram and TikTok.
