Dr. Wesam Amer Warns of a “Lost Generation” as Gaza’s Universities Face Devastation

6 June 2025 · ABC Radio National, Australia · Interviewee

Dr. Wesam Amer spoke with ABC News about the destruction of Gaza’s higher education sector, warning that thousands of Palestinian students risk becoming a “lost generation.” Drawing on his experience as Dean of the Faculty of Communication and Languages at Gaza University, he highlighted the urgent need for rebuilding academic infrastructure and protecting access to education.

Dr. Wesam Amer has called for urgent international action to restore higher education in Gaza, warning that the destruction of universities and academic infrastructure threatens the future of an entire generation of Palestinian students.

In an interview with ABC News and ABC Radio National’s Late Night Live, Dr. Amer discussed the impact of the war on Gaza’s higher education system and the long-term consequences for students, academics, and knowledge production. He argued that the destruction of universities represents more than physical damage to educational institutions, describing it as a direct threat to Palestinian society’s future.

Speaking as Dean of the Faculty of Communication and Languages at Gaza University and a visiting researcher at the University of Cambridge, Dr. Amer warned that Gaza is already facing what he described as a “lost generation” of students and academics whose education has been severely disrupted by the conflict.

During the interview, Dr. Amer emphasized the central role that education plays within Palestinian society. He argued that education is closely linked to dignity, opportunity, identity, and community resilience. He stated that education for Palestinians is not only a pathway to personal advancement but also a vital component of social and cultural continuity.

Dr. Amer also outlined practical steps that could help rebuild the sector. He called for coordinated international efforts to rehabilitate damaged infrastructure, establish mobile learning facilities, expand access to digital academic resources, and strengthen partnerships between Palestinian institutions and universities around the world. These measures, he argued, are essential to ensuring educational continuity while longer-term reconstruction efforts take shape.

The interview contributed to growing international discussions about the future of higher education in Gaza and the challenges facing students seeking to continue their studies amid widespread destruction. Dr. Amer stressed that protecting education must remain a priority in recovery efforts, emphasizing that universities are not only places of learning but also institutions that sustain knowledge, research, and social development.
