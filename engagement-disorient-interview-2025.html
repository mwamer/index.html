Dr. Wesam Amer Highlights the Importance of Education and the Impact of Scholasticide in dis:orient Interview

1 July 2025 · dis:orient, Germany · Interviewee

In an interview with the German platform dis:orient, Dr. Wesam Amer reflected on the destruction of Gaza's education system and the broader implications of scholasticide. Drawing on his experience as an academic from Gaza, he emphasized the central role of education in Palestinian society and underscored the importance of defending knowledge, academic freedom, and the right to speak.

Dr. Wesam Amer has contributed to an international discussion on the future of education in Gaza through an in-depth interview with dis:orient, examining the impact of war on Palestinian educational institutions and the growing relevance of the concept of scholasticide. The interview explored how the destruction of schools, universities, and knowledge systems affects not only education but also identity, cultural continuity, and social resilience.

During the discussion, Dr. Amer described education as a foundational element of Palestinian life. He argued that, particularly in Gaza, education represents far more than formal learning, serving as a source of dignity, hope, resilience, and continuity across generations. According to Dr. Amer, Palestinian families have historically viewed education as one of the most important means of preserving opportunity and identity despite decades of conflict, blockade, and instability.

The interview focused extensively on scholasticide, a term used to describe the systematic destruction of educational institutions and academic life. Dr. Amer explained that the concept helps illuminate how attacks on schools, universities, students, teachers, and research infrastructures affect the long-term future of communities by disrupting the production and transmission of knowledge.

He also stressed the importance of protecting the voices of scholars, journalists, and educators during periods of conflict. In the interview, Dr. Amer argued that academic communities must continue documenting events, preserving knowledge, and contributing to public understanding despite immense challenges. He emphasized that silence and erasure threaten not only individuals but also collective memory and intellectual life.

The conversation forms part of wider international debates about academic freedom, the protection of educational institutions, and the responsibilities of the global scholarly community in responding to threats against higher education and knowledge systems. Through the interview, Dr. Amer contributed to broader efforts to raise awareness about the challenges facing Palestinian education and the long-term consequences of attacks on academic infrastructure.
